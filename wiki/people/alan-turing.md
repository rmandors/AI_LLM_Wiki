# Alan Turing

**Who / when.** British mathematician and computer scientist, 1912–1954 (general knowledge). Defined the Turing machine (1936, general knowledge); worked on wartime code-breaking — his team built the Heath Robinson and Colossus machines in 1943 ([[russell-norvig-aima]] §1.2.6).

**Contribution to AI.**
- Lectured on machine intelligence from **1947**; his **1950** paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" introduced the **[[turing-test]]**, and also **machine learning**, **genetic algorithms** and **reinforcement learning**, and answered objections to AI ([[russell-norvig-aima]] §1.3.1).
- Argued it would be easier to build human-level AI by creating *learning* machines and teaching them than by programming intelligence by hand — the idea behind learning agents ([[agent-architectures]]; R&N §2.4.6).
- Outlined the first chess program (Turing et al. 1953) ([[russell-norvig-aima]] §1.2.6 fn. 10) — an ancestor of [[minimax]] game playing.
- Warned that achieving human-level AI might not be good for humanity (§1.3.1).
- Course: dated 1950 on [[slides-01-introduction-to-ai]] slide 5. The ACM Turing Award is named after him (general knowledge).
