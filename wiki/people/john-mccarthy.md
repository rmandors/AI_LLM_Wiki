# John McCarthy

**Who / when.** American computer scientist (1927–2011, general knowledge), at Dartmouth in 1955–56, then MIT, then founder of the Stanford AI lab in 1963 ([[russell-norvig-aima]] §1.3.1–1.3.2). Turing Award 1971 for foundations of AI based on representation and reasoning (§1.3).

**Contribution to AI.**
- **Named the field**: the 1955 Dartmouth proposal is the first official use of "artificial intelligence" (R&N §1.3.1 fn. 12).
- **Dartmouth workshop, 1956**, organised with Minsky, Shannon and Rochester — the "birth of AI" ([[slides-01-introduction-to-ai]], slide 7).
- **LISP (1958)**, MIT AI Lab Memo No. 1: based on lambda calculus and recursion, dynamic types, list processing, prefix calls `(f a1 a2 a3)` ([[slides-01-introduction-to-ai]], slide 6); the dominant AI language for 30 years (R&N §1.3.2). The slide gives 1955–1958.
- **"Programs with Common Sense" (1958)**: the *Advice Taker*, a hypothetical program that uses general knowledge in logical axioms to derive plans — the founding idea of knowledge representation and reasoning, the line leading to [[first-order-logic]] and [[prolog]] (R&N §1.3.2).
- Called early AI the "Look, Ma, no hands!" era (§1.3.2).
